# Main alternative specification: logit IV with expense ratio × "cheap fund"

This file is self-contained. It gives the recommended alternative demand model for the index market, its definition, Table 2 with the interaction at three cutoffs, robustness to the cutoff, and the implied elasticities of the 15 Table 5 funds. Everything else (other interactions, log price, the one-random-coefficient BLP, Table A7, and the further robustness checks of this specification) is in `OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`. The paper's own numbers were never changed. All numbers come from scripts in `code/explore/` with logs in `logs/`; the tables are generated by `code/explore/make_table2_alt.py` from `logs/explore/*.csv`.

## 1. The specification

Same samples, controls (past 1-year return, log age, tracking error), fund and month fixed effects, and SE clustered by month as Table 2. The only change is one extra term in the mean utility:

  δ_jt = α · p_jt + α_c · (p_jt × cheap_j) + controls + fund FE + month FE + ξ_jt

- **Estimator:** logit IV (Table 2 column 2 with the interaction). Instruments: the notebook's Hausman expense-ratio IV and Hausman IV × cheap. The Hausman IV (`BLP_IV_hausman_exp_ratio`) arrives precomputed in the data, and no notebook builds it. Reverse-engineered (`code/explore/step23_hausman_reconstruct.py`), it is the previous-month-AUM-weighted average expense ratio of the **same fund family's funds that track other indices** in the same month (family = `firm_f_old`). The reconstruction matches the column within 0.0005 percentage points in 83% of estimation-sample fund-months (correlation 0.9997). It is undefined for the 7% with no family identifier, where the column's construction is unknown. Two endogenous terms, two instruments: exactly identified. The first-stage F reported is the Sanderson–Windmeijer F, minimum over the two endogenous terms, cluster-robust.
- **No random coefficient.** The paper's own index-market BLP (Table 2 col 3) has σ = 0.027 (SE 0.013), i.e. no detectable heterogeneity in the price coefficient, and once the interaction is added σ is not identified by any instrument set in the data (details in `OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`, section 4). The logit IV extension therefore loses no heterogeneity the paper had found.
- **Leveraged funds (Panel B): no interaction.** In the paper's A7 the leveraged broad-based interaction is not significant, and the logit screen found no significant fee-tier interaction there either. Panel B stays as in Table 2.

### Definition of "cheap" (`code/explore/tiers.py`)

For each month of the **full raw index file** (Jan 1994 – Dec 2025, all index funds with a recorded fee, before the sample's share filter), compute the q-th percentile of expense ratios across funds. A fund is **cheap** if its expense ratio in its **first raw month** is at or below that month's cutoff; ties count as cheap.

- The status is fixed per fund, so fund FE absorb its level and only the slope changes.
- It is predetermined: 54% of sample funds first appear in the raw data before the Jan 2010 start of the estimation window (median entry 2009). The estimation window is Jan 2010 – Aug 2024 (166 months, N = 108,300).
- It is measured relative to the market at the time, so a fund launched at 0.20% in 2001 is judged against 2001 fees, not 2024 fees.
- Cutoffs reported: q = 10th, 15th and 20th percentiles (20%, 31% and 38% of sample funds cheap). The 20th percentile is the suggested main cutoff (strongest first stage, smoothest neighbourhood); the 10th and 15th are robustness.
- **Repriced funds.** Several large funds launched at a mid-range fee and were repriced to the cheap end later (the iShares "Core" repricing in 2012, the SPDR Portfolio fee cuts in 2017). They are classified by their launch fee:

  | Fund | First raw month | Fee then (%) | 10th / 15th / 20th cutoff that month | Fee now (%) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | iShares Core US Aggregate Bond (AGG) | Apr 2004 | 0.20 | 0.150 / 0.200 / 0.250 | 0.03 |
  | iShares Core S&P Mid-Cap | Jul 2001 | 0.20 | 0.180 / 0.208 / 0.250 | 0.05 |
  | iShares Russell 1000 Growth | Jul 2001 | 0.20 | 0.180 / 0.208 / 0.250 | 0.18 |
  | SPDR Portfolio S&P 500 | Jun 2006 | 0.21 | 0.121 / 0.180 / 0.230 | 0.02 |
  | iShares Core S&P 500 | Jul 2001 | 0.09 | 0.180 / 0.208 / 0.250 | 0.03 |

  So AGG is "not cheap" at the 10th percentile but cheap at the 15th and 20th; SPDR Portfolio S&P 500 is cheap only at the 20th. This is the price of a predetermined tier: definitions that use later fees (rolling status, lagged-fee percentile) failed the exogeneity checks (`OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`, section 2). It is also a reason to prefer the 15th or 20th percentile: at the 10th, these funds' large repricings are within-fund fee changes that identify the *non-cheap* slope.

### Why a fee-level interaction is what the data want

With market shares this small (median 0.037%, max 4.97%), the logit own elasticity is essentially α·p. |e| = 1 at p = 0.03% needs α ≈ −33, and −25 to −52 across the 2–4 basis point funds; the pooled Table 2 estimate is −5.6 (logit IV) to −8.3 (3 RC). Every cutoff below gives the cheap group a slope 4–11 times the rest's: the demand curve is much steeper at the low-fee end, and the tier is the linear model's way of expressing that.


### Important: the Hausman IV is fragile (`OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`, section 6)

- **Where Table 2's −5.6 comes from.** It is driven by the 7% of fund-months (112 mostly insurance-channel funds) that have no family identifier, and for which the instrument's construction is unknown. On the other 93% the same instrument gives **+7.2** (upward-sloping demand), and lead tests reject it (t up to 20).
- **A better-behaved instrument.** The median fee of the same family's same-share-class funds *outside the fund's asset class* has a first-stage F of 150–330, negative slopes in every split, and survives asset-class × month fixed effects.
- **The main spec re-estimated with it, fund + month FE, 20th percentile, 97,429 fund-months:**
  - non-cheap −6.12 (0.85);
  - cheap total −28.8;
  - Table 5 funds above |e| = 1: 7 of 15;
  - average own elasticity −2.7;
  - with asset-class × month FE: −3.80 (0.66) and −24.0, with 5 of 15 above 1.
- **Full report on that specification:** `MAIN_REPORT_sharecl_hausman.md`.
- **What survives.** The cheap-fund gap does, and the non-cheap slope is now precisely estimated. But the Table 5 count is lower than the 11 of 15 reported below with the original instrument. Which instrument to adopt is a decision for the authors. The tables below still use the paper's instrument.

### Reading the table

- **Cheap funds' total price coefficient is −41 to −69, against −6 to −16 for the rest.** The interaction is significant at every cutoff (t = 2.8, 4.2, 7.8). The non-cheap coefficient is imprecise (SE 4–8); the gap is what is well estimated.
- **No fund-month has upward-sloping demand** at any cutoff.
- **Average own elasticity −3.2 to −6.3** (Table 2: −1.6). Negative implied marginal cost (firm Bertrand) falls from 52% of fund-months to 5–31%.
- **Costs:** the controls move (age 0.57 → 0.65–0.83; tracking error −0.45 → −1.3 to −1.8), and the weakest first stage is F = 11–18 (Table 2: 34).
- **Logit OLS (1′) barely moves**, as OLS is attenuated in Table 2 col 1.

- The **cheap** total moves smoothly with the cutoff (−69, −61, −41, −30, −22 from the 10th to the 30th percentile) with F ≥ 10.9 throughout: as the group widens to include less cheap funds its average slope flattens, as it should.

### Split-sample diagnostic: Table 2 col 2 run separately on cheap and non-cheap funds (`code/explore/step20_split_sample_iv.py`, `logs/explore_step20_split_sample_iv.log`)

What this shows:
- **Cheap funds: the instrument works.** For cheap funds, when the Hausman IV rises their fee rises and their share falls sharply. The reduced form has t ≈ −20 to −34 at every cutoff, so a zero price coefficient is rejected decisively even allowing for a weak first stage. The split-sample IV is −37 to −57, close to the interacted model's cheap total (−41 to −69).
- **Non-cheap funds: the instrument barely moves their fees.** The first-stage F is 2.6 to 6.2, although the Hausman IV varies more within these funds than within cheap funds. Their own IV slope is −12 to −13 at the 10th and 15th percentiles, with SEs of 6–9, and +4.7 (3.1) at the 20th. By the reduced-form test the non-cheap slope is negative at the 10th and 15th (t −2.9 and −4.2) but not distinguishable from zero at the 20th (t +1.8).
- **This is why the non-cheap slope has been fragile.** It took the values −8, −19, −28, +6 and +14 depending on the sample and specification, because the Hausman IV identifies it only weakly. The cheap slope, which drives the Table 5 funds, is the well-identified part. In the pooled model (2′), the non-cheap coefficient borrows strength from the common month FE and controls; that is why its Sanderson–Windmeijer F (18.3) looks better than the split-sample F.
- Table 2's single −5.6 is dominated by the cheap funds: their first-stage coefficient is three to ten times the non-cheap funds'.
- **Other instruments do not fix the non-cheap slope** (`OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`, section 5). I screened all 27 instruments the coauthor built. The cost-side variants give a slope near zero or positive. The rival and new-product counts have strong first stages but disagree with each other (−17 to +6), and the most stable of them, the new-product counts (−7 to −11), show the sign pattern of business stealing that a plain logit leaves in the error. The cheap slope does not depend on this: it is identified by the Hausman IV alone. A nested logit with tracked-index nests, which models within-index business stealing, does not identify the non-cheap slope either (section 5b of the same file): it turns positive at the 10th and 20th percentiles, and the overidentification tests still reject.

### Exogeneity

The tier is fixed at the fund's first raw month, before the estimation window for most funds, so it cannot respond to demand shocks inside the window; fund FE absorb its level. Its interaction with the Hausman IV inherits the paper's exclusion restriction (other markets' fees are uncorrelated with the fund's within-month demand shock). Every time-varying alternative tried (rolling cheap status, lagged AUM tercile, lagged fee tercile, lagged-fee percentile) failed lead tests: past demand shocks predicted the classifier, with ξ̂ persistent within fund (AR(1) ≈ 0.98). Details in `OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`, section 2. The closest to passing is status from the fee 60 months earlier (section 2c). At the 20th percentile it gives 13 of 15 Table 5 funds above 1 and a cheap total of −48, which makes it a useful robustness exhibit. It still carries the classification-date demand shock through a within-fund autocorrelation of 0.12. It also adds a large status main effect (+2.8) and triples the non-cheap slope.

## 5. How to report it

- Add column (2′) at the 20th percentile to Table 2 Panel A as the alternative demand model, with the 10th and 15th (and optionally 25th/30th) percentiles in a robustness table. Panel B unchanged.
- Footnote: a random coefficient on the expense ratio is not identified once the interaction is included (the GMM objective is flat in σ; the paper's own σ = 0.027 is not rejected, Hansen J p = 0.088), and every estimator that lets σ float gives upward-sloping demand for non-cheap funds, so the column is logit IV. If a random-coefficient column is wanted, a column with σ held at the Table 2 col 3 value exists but is numerically identical to (2′) and should be labelled as a sensitivity exhibit, not an estimated BLP (`OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`, section 4).
- State the caveats: the non-cheap slope is weakly identified (split-sample first-stage F 2.6–6.2, section 4) and depends mainly on Vanguard's fee changes (`OTHER_SPECS_explored.md`, section 2b); the tier is set at entry, so repriced funds (AGG, SPDR Portfolio S&P 500) are classified by their launch fee.
- Under this model the cheapest Table 5 funds have |e| of 1.2–3.5 at the 20th percentile instead of 0.16–0.33, so the Lerner-index implication of Table 5 (marginal cost below zero for the cheapest funds) is substantially reduced but not removed for the 0.02% funds (SPDR Portfolio S&P 500 −0.81, Vanguard TSM Inst. Plus −0.78).
