# Table 2 benchmark and Hausman instrument exclusions

Coauthor note and research AI handoff. November 10, 2026.

The research objective is to use the paper's Table 2 data and sample as a frozen benchmark and investigate whether additional exclusions from the Hausman instrument's donor pool can mitigate shared demand shocks. The completed tests justify investigating broader economic exclusions. They have not established that any tested instrument satisfies the exclusion restriction.

The most informative comparison keeps the original Table 2 demand specification fixed and changes only Hausman IV0. Broader exposure exclusions can preserve useful first stages and downward IV-logit demand. Same-type matching alone can reverse the passive price coefficient, and BLP1 results remain sensitive to information and economic-sign problems. A negative coefficient, strong first stage, converged solver or favorable conduct result cannot independently validate exclusion.

**The frozen benchmark.** The benchmark is the supplied estimation samples, with all original rows, order, identifiers, shares, prices, units and sample membership preserved:

The passive sample omits ten months in its observed date range; the leveraged sample is Bull only. Preserve the prepared age units, absolute tracking error and original shares and denominators. Prices are annual expense ratios in percentage points. Fee overlays, money-market exclusions, revised outside options, new classifications and alternative samples are separately labeled sensitivities.

The four original linear characteristics are expense ratio, tracking error, past one-year return and log fund age, with product and month fixed effects. The primary OLS outcome is log product TNA. IV logit uses IV0 alone. BLP1 adds a random price coefficient and uses IV0 plus the original market-specific IV1: the passive new-product instrument and the leveraged rival-funds instrument. The historical BLP3 specification uses IV0 through IV3 and is an archived reference rather than part of the current exclusion grid. Construct minimal model frames because PyBLP automatically discovers numbered instrument columns.

Intended corrected inference uses monthly clustered standard errors and robust two-step GMM weighting. Clustering the weighting matrix is a separate sensitivity. The paper's authors confirmed that its printed BLP standard errors were mistakenly unclustered. Preserve those printed targets as historical evidence. Other discrepancies remain, including the passive IV price SE of 1.310205 versus the paper's 0.131, a passive BLP3 loading-sign difference and small leveraged rounding/inference differences. Matching the printed table does not certify convergence. Sources: [S1] and [S2].

**What the original Hausman instrument does.** Original IV0 uses current fees of other funds in the same historical family and month, excluding the focal CRSP class and exact benchmark, with prepared lag-AUM weights. Leveraged donors also match Bull/Bear direction. The donor universe is the full historical source panel, including observations outside the Table 2 target sample: 675,551 passive source rows and 56,450 leveraged source rows. Both original IV0 vectors now reconstruct on every frozen target within absolute tolerance 1e-10.

Literal source conventions matter. Observed donor lag AUM can remain in the denominator when its current fee is missing and contributes no numerator. Blank passive family strings match one another, affecting 7,771 target observations; this is historical code behavior rather than evidence of common management. Seventeen leveraged target rows have historical source-family `Other` but different family labels in the later frozen export. The accepted leveraged reconstruction uses the archive's saved imputation intermediate; regenerating that intermediate from a different archived snapshot does not reproduce every fee. Preserve these distinctions when reproducing the baseline, and version any cleanup separately. Source: [S3].

**The completed exclusion experiment.** Four donor exclusions are crossed with three share-access pools, giving twelve candidates per market. The exclusions are exact benchmark only; exact benchmark plus return cluster; exact benchmark plus category10; and exact benchmark plus category20. The pools are all types, the target's own type, and other classified types. Type distinguishes ETF, retail mutual fund and institutional mutual fund access/vehicle categories; it does not identify ultimate investor ownership.

The category10 and category20 rules are project classifications of underlying exposure, grounded in source evidence. They are not recovered vendor assignments. Category10 is coarser and, on the same donor universe, excludes at least the corresponding category20 donors. The return clusters and exposure categories are different partitions. Existing tests compare them separately; a union excluding both the target's cluster and category has not yet been tested. Unsupported categories remain explicitly unknown.

Each construction has a raw instrument, missing when no valid construction exists, and a full-sample hybrid that uses actual original IV0 on unsupported rows. Only IV0 changes in the Hausman-only demand grid. Controls, fixed effects, samples, original BLP1 IV1, integration and numerical settings remain fixed. All 24 hybrid IV-logit fits converge. **Donor support is a substantive constraint.** Leveraged cross-type raw IVs exist on only 4,410-4,411 of 15,401 rows. Their full-sample hybrids retain original IV0 on more than 71% of observations. Common raw support across all twelve candidates is 92,680 passive and 4,410 leveraged rows. On restricted leveraged supports, some original auxiliary IVs are month-constant and absorbed by month effects; the corrected diagnostics explicitly mark 42 requested two-/four-IV cases unidentified rather than treating rounding noise as variation.

A raw-support comparison can separate sample composition from instrument construction by estimating the original-IV control on exactly the same rows. It remains a restricted-support diagnostic alongside the full benchmark. A hybrid preserves the full sample but cannot be called a pure stricter-exclusion instrument. When a raw candidate is undefined on some paper rows, the current construction cannot deliver a pure full-sample estimate without a further methodological or data change. Sources: [S3]-[S5].

**What the numerical and instrument-subset tests add.** In the Hausman-only grid, 18/24 hybrid BLP1 fits converge and eight pass local-information checks. None passes convergence, information and economic-sign checks together. All eight information-passing hybrids have some upward own-price responses. Failed fits retain unavailable downstream outcomes. The exploratory report's certification verifies the reported results and qualifications; the original strict baseline convergence gate remains failed and was not converted into a scientific pass.

Earlier tests of all seven BLP1 IV subsets containing IV0 in each market give fourteen numerically converged cases, but several fail useful local first-order information. Even full numerical rank need not imply strong information. Rank loss in the squared information matrix alone does not prove exact structural nonidentification; inspect raw derivatives, scaling, singular values and standardized conditioning. Finite native SEs can be diagnostic rather than support ordinary Wald inference.

Changing auxiliary instruments can materially change the estimates and implied economic behavior. Passive four-IV logit reproduces the BLP3 elasticity near -2.404 without random heterogeneity. Thus the original BLP1-to-BLP3 contrast does not isolate added age/return heterogeneity. Leveraged instrument subsets can produce similar average elasticities but different derivative tails and conduct conclusions. This is why an exclusion comparison should preserve each model's original auxiliary IV subset. Sources: [S5] and [S6].

**What cheap and breadth tests add.** The larger completed frozen-paper-sample interaction report contains 120 displays representing 88 canonical cases. All pass its numerical gate, while 21 BLP1 cases fail local information and 44 demand cases have upward-own-response qualifications. A negative mean price coefficient or average elasticity does not imply that every observed own response is downward.

The Table A7 exercise reproduces all twenty OLS/IV coefficients and four elasticities at printed precision, with three SE discrepancies. Source-grounded breadth repairs address errors including SPY classification and currency modifiers applied to diversified underlying exposures. Repaired cross-asset labels give downward OLS/IV responses in both full samples. Passive BLP1 still fails information; leveraged BLP1 retains 3,060 upward responses despite negative group means. Classification repair improves measurement and can support an economically motivated heterogeneity specification, but it does not validate Hausman exclusion. Earlier passive sample revisions, including the 97,399-row exercise, and alternative cheap-reference universes remain separate experiments. Sources: [S7] and [S8].

**Three median instruments must be distinguished.** The direct original-donor median replaces the original weighted aggregation while retaining original donor eligibility and exclusions. It has full raw support in both paper samples with no fallback. In the repaired-breadth interaction model, passive IV specialized/broad slopes are +3.105/-1.886. Passive BLP1 becomes downward but fails information; leveraged BLP1 reduces upward responses from 3,060 to 2,142 without eliminating them. A median changes aggregation rather than adding exclusions.

The older supplied passive median patch is a different construction with 430 original-IV0 fallbacks. Its leveraged results retain original IV0, so they are not new leveraged median estimates. Its passive BLP1 fails second-step convergence. The actual received V2 instrument is a third construction: a current-fee unweighted median within recorded family, month and share type, excluding the target's six-asset group and retaining money-market donor and target exclusions. Those target exclusions affect 437 frozen passive observations. Unlike the original IV, its native rule does not require lag AUM or the original leveraged direction predicate. Sources: [S9] and [S10].

**What explains the historical V2 contrast.** The matched evidence shows substantial specification sensitivity. On identical 96,047 rows, with identical approved-type IV values, fees, shares, controls and fixed effects, the previous price-by-cheap and IV-by-cheap specification gives non-cheap/cheap-total slopes -3.7518/-24.9792. Jointly removing those interactions produces a common slope +0.0020 (SE 0.3240). These are different coefficient estimands; this comparison does not show which specification is correct.

Within the separate no-interaction grid, median versus lag-AUM weighting changes the passive IV coefficient from +0.1743 (SE 0.3305) to -1.0238 (SE 0.4232) on the same 96,814 rows. Historical V2 itself used a median, so weighting does not explain that historical contrast. The tested support changes do not restore the favorable no-interaction IV sign. Replacing V2's inherited share-type policy with approved CRSP-supported types preserves downward slopes in the interacted design. V2's six asset groups and the project's 10/20 categories are not nested; six-group V2 can exclude more economically related donors despite having fewer labels. Sources: [S10] and [S11].

**The exclusion argument that remains.** Other-asset products in the same family or channel may still share advertising, reputation, distribution, investor demand and fee-policy shocks. Lag-AUM weights can also reflect persistent demand. Different benchmark names, different categories or being outside the selected target sample do not establish independence from focal demand shocks. Broader exposure exclusions plausibly mitigate shared investment-demand shocks, while cross-type donors examine segment separation. Neither removes family-wide shocks automatically. The exactly identified logit equations provide no overidentification test of instrument validity.

Brown, Egan, Jeon, Jin and Wu's July 2026 index-fund draft explicitly discusses family advertising, reputation, manager quality and demand spillovers as threats to same-manager other-market fee instruments (pp. 24-25). Egan, MacKay and Yang's September 2025 portfolio draft conditions its argument on plan-by-year and category-by-year controls and considers distinct investor segments and cost shifters (section 3.3.2 and Appendix C). These are economic motivations for further checks, not evidence that their identifying assumptions automatically transfer to Table 2. Primary sources: [Brown et al.](https://chuqingjin.github.io/files/brown_et_al_Why_Do_Index_Funds_Have_Market_Power_202607.pdf) and [Egan, MacKay and Yang](https://alexandermackay.org/files/What%20Drives%20Variation%20in%20Investor%20Portfolios%20-%20Estimating%20the%20Roles%20of%20Beliefs%20and%20Risk%20Preferences.pdf).

**Research priorities.** Keep the original Table 2 benchmark as the reference and predeclare new exclusions using exposure and institutional evidence. A useful next exclusion grid would compare broad exposure rules and an explicit cluster/category union, crossing donor types separately from aggregation choices. Record raw support, donor counts and concentration, uncertain family/category assignments and every fallback before interpreting coefficients.

Start the interpretation with IV logit, then assess BLP1 separately through convergence, local information, own/cross derivatives and covariance diagnostics. Complement exclusions with separately labeled checks of family/channel and exposure-time shocks. Additional fixed effects change the specification and may absorb identifying variation, so check surviving instrument rank. Distinct-segment donors and independently motivated cost shifters are further research directions. Classifications and donor rules should be chosen without screening for a desired slope or conduct p-value.

Supply and conduct follow the demand assessment. Instrument subsets, maintained scale corrections and markup caps can change costs and confidence sets. Capped calculations do not validate the original first-order conditions, and conditional conduct inference does not automatically propagate demand-estimation uncertainty. Preserve raw outcomes and qualifications; favorable costs or a preferred conduct model cannot select the IV. Source: [S14].
